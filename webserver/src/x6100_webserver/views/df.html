Direction finder

The radio's page I with a KrakenSDR on its HOST port, at the browser's size: the bearing, the method's spectrum round the array, the band and every channel, and all of page I's settings (the same ones: a change here shows on the radio's screen too). While this page is open the radio keeps direction finding running, page I on its screen or not, and lets the Kraken go about 20 seconds after you close the page. Receive only.

Tune

Follow takes the radio's own dial (A) or B's frequency as they change. A click on the spectrum below tunes there.

Other targets

TargetMHzBearingFitLevel, dBKept up

Up to three more bearings at once in the Kraken's passband, each with its own filter and squelch, on the radio's spare cores. Watch the target too keeps the main target's frequency as another one, so the main one can be tuned elsewhere; a shift-click on the spectrum puts one there. Kept up: the share of blocks its core kept up with (under 90 %: the processor is short, fewer or a lower rate helps). A target outside the passband waits till the Kraken is tuned back round it.

Settings

The log files

The log: each bearing over the squelch, two a second at most, in a CSV file a day on the card (df_log): the time, the frequency, the bearing, the heading and the compass bearing, its fit, and gpsd's position when there is one. It stays on the radio.

Array

Every layout at MHz

LayoutSize, cm
s 0.33 (0.2-0.5)		Apart, cm
(widest)		Res.

Five antennas set out in any of these, sized for the target. Size: a circle's (and 4+1's) radius, a line's (and the L's) spacing, the minimum-redundancy line's unit; KrakenRF's spacing over the wavelength s under 0.5 (over it the bearing can be ambiguous), over about 0.2 (under it coarse), 0.33 its usual choice, which Use sets (r a radius, d a spacing). Apart: neighbours, and in brackets the widest pair, at 0.33; Res.: the resolution there, roughly; even: ROOT-MUSIC and smoothing work on it. UCA: KrakenRF's circles, all round. ULA: a line across the way you face, 180 degrees, front and back alike, best straight out. 4+1: four round one in the middle, the Adcock square with its sense antenna. L-5: two short lines at a right angle, all round with no mirror. MRL-5: a minimum-redundancy line (Moffet, 1968), antennas at 0, 1, 4, 7 and 9 units: every spacing from 1 to 9 units, so the width of a ten-antenna line from five; still a line, and uneven, so MUSIC on the grid only (no ROOT-MUSIC, smoothing or FB). Equal antenna cables for all of them, within a centimetre.

Antenna calibration

The noise source lines up the Kraken's receivers, not what is in front of them: antenna cables of unequal lengths, unlike whips. A transmitter at a bearing you know measures that. Put one you may use there (a handheld on low power on a band you are licensed for, an ISM-band device) on the target's frequency, ten wavelengths or more away in the clear, at the bearing above from the array's forward (0: straight out in front; a line's 0 is straight out from it), key it, and press Calibrate on it: a few seconds of it over the squelch are taken, each antenna's phase against what the layout gives there. It is kept on the card for this frequency and used within 2 % of it (a cable's phase grows with the frequency): take one a band. Equal cables are still best; this mends what they cannot.

Custom layout

AntForward, cmLeft, cm

Any layout of two to five antennas: where each stands from the middle, forward (ANT 0's heading, up on the drawing) and left, in cm. Drag one on the drawing or type it. Save and use writes it to the card (df_array.txt) and picks CUSTOM, here and on page I. The bearing is only as good as the positions: measure them to the antennas' feed points.

GPS (gpsd)

Bluetooth GPS

An NMEA GPS on Bluetooth (a Garmin GLO 2 and the like), for gpsd beside Bluetooth CAT: the radio keeps connecting to it, so switching it on is all it takes. Pair it first in the radio's Settings > Bluetooth (Scan, then press it).
